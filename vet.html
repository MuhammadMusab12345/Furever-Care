FIRST FEW MINUTES

What To Do — and What Not To

Simple actions until you reach a vet. Staying calm helps your pet stay calm too.

Do

  • Stay calm and speak softly — your pet reacts to your energy.
  • Keep your pet warm, still and supported while you call for help.
  • Note down symptoms, timing and what happened, if anything.
  • Call the helpline or clinic before moving or transporting your pet.
  • Use a secure carrier or firm board to move an injured pet.
  • Muzzle gently only if it's safe to do so — pain can cause biting.

Don't

  • Don't give any human medicine without a vet's direction.
  • Don't try to induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to.
  • Don't apply ice directly onto a wound or burn.
  • Don't force water or food into an unconscious or dazed pet.
  • Don't wait to "see if it gets better" — call immediately instead.
  • Don't restrain a seizing pet or put your hand near its mouth.
KNOW THE SIGNS

Emergency Symptoms to Watch For

Recognising these early can buy your pet precious extra minutes.

Poisoning

Drooling, vomiting, sudden weakness, tremors or seizures after eating something unusual.

Call the Poison Helpline now and bring the packaging.

Severe Bleeding / Injury

Continuous bleeding, visible wounds, limping or refusal to put weight on a limb.

Apply firm, clean pressure and get to a clinic fast.

Breathing Difficulty

Rapid or laboured breathing, blue-tinged gums, wheezing or choking sounds.

Keep airway clear and rush to the nearest vet.

Seizures

Uncontrolled shaking, loss of consciousness, paddling legs or drooling.

Clear the area, don't restrain, and time the episode.

Heatstroke

Heavy panting, thick drooling, bright red gums, weakness or collapse.

Move to shade, cool paws and belly with water, call vet.

Choking

Pawing at the mouth, distress, blue-tinged tongue or gagging without result.

Check the mouth carefully and get help immediately.
